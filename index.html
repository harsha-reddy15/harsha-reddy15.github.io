1912 Voice AI Helpline

● Live 24/7

Multilingual voice agent for the 1912 electricity helpline · serves 12M+ consumers

The problem

The 1912 helpline handles power outages, billing questions and complaints for millions of consumers, many of whom speak only Telugu or Hindi and switch languages mid-sentence. Callers waited around 8 minutes for a resolution, and the line needed to work around the clock.

Architecture

Everything runs on-premise. No call audio or consumer data leaves the client's infrastructure.

Key engineering decisions

Two speech models, routed by languageWhisper for English, an Indic Conformer for Telugu and Hindi, with debounced language detection so callers can switch language mid-call.
Self-hosted LLM on one GPUFP8-quantised Gemma 4 31B served with vLLM keeps latency at 1.4 s and avoids per-call API costs and data leaving the premises.
Humans for safety, by designFallen wires, shocks and fire risk skip the bot and go straight to a person, with non-interruptible critical messages.
Real-world conversation controlLoop detection (n-gram + cosine similarity), silence handling, barge-in, speaker verification and spoken-digit capture of consumer numbers.

Quality & reliability

  • Automated evaluation suite: scripted multi-turn test calls graded by an LLM-as-judge, so prompt and flow changes are checked before release.
  • Release gate and canary checks: deploys are blocked on new test failures, and new versions must match the old version's P95 latency before full rollout.
  • Resilience: circuit breaker on backend APIs and automatic LLM failover.
  • Continuous improvement: I review real call transcripts to find unhandled intents and ship prompt, flow and routing fixes.

DTR Nameplate OCR

● 1M+ nameplates processed

Extracting transformer data from field photos for a state-wide survey

The problem

Field teams photographed the nameplates of more than a million distribution transformers. Each nameplate's serial number, capacity, currents, manufacturer and dates had to be entered into a database, and the photos were often blurred, angled or badly lit.

Two phases, two architectures

Why the architecture changed

Phase 1: optimise for speedDuring the peak field survey, a single-pass vision model on GCP, scaled out to 16 CPU instances, was the fastest way to keep up with incoming photos.
Phase 2: optimise for costOnce volume dropped, paying per image no longer made sense. I moved reading to PaddleOCR and structuring to the Gemma 4 model we already ran for the voice bot.
